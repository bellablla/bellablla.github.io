I'm Isabella, a junior American Studies major. I have some experience with sewing and embroidery. I was given a needlepoint kit by a family friend in middle school, and although I never completed it, I learned how to create a complex design by layering different threads to add depth and variation to the colors. I've sometimes mended my own clothes, adding a new waistband to a vintage skirt or sewing up a ripped seam. I've also tried knitting, and I want to learn how to weave. One boring fact about me is that I've been drinking a lot of tea recently.
I don't have much experience programming, and growing up, computers were my older brother's specialty. He built his first computer in third grade with help from his friends father. He used github and competed in hackathons, and sometime's we'd all go together to coder dojo on Sundays. I learned how to use scratch, and how to drag and drop commands to make a robot dance, or to create a simple game, but I never learned a programming language. I accompanied my brother when he bought a Raspberry Pi, and we once made a circuit out of a potato. Other than that, I don't have experience with circuitry or Arduino. 
In this class, I hope to learn the basics of how hardware works. How do we create a closed circuit, or program the Arduino that causes a light to flash, or a speaker to emit a sound? When something goes wrong, how do we fix it? How do we build systems that are reliable efficient?
